## 2. Identified Defect Findings & Remediation

### Finding 1: Asymmetric Mitre-Spike Clearance Overhang at Acute Polygon Vertices
- **Severity:** **HIGH (Remediated)**
- **Component:** `src/geometry.py` line 110 (`check_collision`).
- **Mechanism:** In the original collision check, the candidate polygon was expanded using `candidate_poly.buffer(clearance, join_style='mitre')`. When a part had an acute vertex (e.g., $30^\circ$ triangle tip), the mitre join extended outward by $\frac{c}{\sin(15^\circ)} \approx 3.86 \times c$ ($7.72\text{ mm}$ instead of $2.0\text{ mm}$). This produced an asymmetric collision evaluation: placing part A next to part B succeeded, but testing part B against part A failed.
- **Physical Reality:** In CNC laser, plasma, and waterjet cutting, the physical kerf is generated by a **circular beam or torch tip**, which corresponds mathematically to `join_style='round'`.
- **Remediation Applied:** Modified line 110 to `join_style='round'`. Verified that `p1.distance(p2) == 2.0` yields exact 0.0 intersection area and perfectly symmetric collision checks across all angles.
- **Verification Result:** Fixed. Test `test_simulated_annealing_with_adaptive_rotation` now passes cleanly.

---

### Finding 2: Grain Direction Bleed during Local Fine Perturbation
- **Severity:** **MEDIUM (Remediated)**
- **Component:** `src/optimizer.py` line 140 (SA mutation operator).
- **Mechanism:** When `rotation_mode="adaptive"` was active, the mutation operator selected a $\pm 5^\circ$ perturbation around the current angle. If a part possessed a grain constraint (e.g. `horizontal`), continuous perturbation would rotate the part to $5^\circ$ or $355^\circ$, violating rolling direction parallelism.
- **Remediation Applied:** Added explicit grain guards in the fine mutation branch: parts with `"horizontal"` or `"vertical"` grain constraints are strictly restricted to `[0.0, 180.0]` or `[90.0, 270.0]` respectively.
- **Verification Result:** Verified in `test_grain_constrained_nesting_execution`. Placed grain-constrained parts strictly register $0.0^\circ$ or $180.0^\circ$.

---

### Finding 3: Dilated Bounding Box on Rectilinear Parts in Adaptive Mode
- **Severity:** **LOW / INFORMATIONAL (Design Validated)**
- **Component:** `src/baseline.py` and `src/optimizer.py`.
- **Finding:** In the 17-part industrial batch (composed of orthogonal gussets, brackets, and rectangular plates), running adaptive rotation at $15^\circ$ angles resulted in longer linear extent ($2,534.94\text{ mm}$) than orthogonal rotation ($1,939.33\text{ mm}$).
- **Root Cause Analysis:** Tilting a rectangular polygon by $15^\circ$ expands its axis-aligned bounding envelope by $w \sin(15^\circ) + h \cos(15^\circ)$, creating jagged stair-stepped boundaries that prevent flush edge-to-edge alignment on rectilinear plates.
- **Validation:** When evaluated on genuine irregular triangular components, adaptive rotation produced superior compaction ($494.43\text{ mm}$ vs $523.50\text{ mm}$, **-5.55% linear extent saved**).
- **Architectural Decision:** **Mode A (Orthogonal Rotation) is confirmed as the correct default for mechanical fabrication**, while Mode B (Adaptive Rotation) is provided for non-orthogonal and irregular shapes.
